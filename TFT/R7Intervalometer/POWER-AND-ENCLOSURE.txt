POWER SWITCH AND ENCLOSURE — 9 OCTOBER 2026

Assumption: one lithium cell, protected TP4056 board with B+/B- and OUT+/OUT-,
5 V boost converter, ESP32 Dev Module, display powered from ESP32's supply.
Confirm the actual fitted modules before changing wiring. The reported condition
that only disconnecting the cell restores operation has not been diagnosed.

NORMAL ON/OFF SWITCH
Use a maintained/latching power switch in the protected positive supply BEFORE
the boost converter:

  Cell + -------------------- TP4056 B+
  Cell - -------------------- TP4056 B-

  TP4056 OUT+ --- ON/OFF ----- Boost IN+
  TP4056 OUT- ---------------- Boost IN-

  Boost OUT+ (regulated 5 V) - ESP32 5V/VIN
  Boost OUT- ---------------- ESP32 GND
  ESP32 3V3/GND -------------- LCD supply (for a 3.3 V-rated LCD)

This cuts power to the converter, controller and LCD together. It leaves the cell
connected to the charger/protection board, so charging remains available with
the load switched OFF. With a basic TP4056 board, charge with the load OFF; it
is not a complete simultaneous charge-and-load power-path manager.

Do not bypass the protection by powering the project directly from B+/B-.
Do not connect protected OUT- to B- externally. Choose a switch rated for the
converter's battery-side input current and startup surge, not merely the ESP32's
average current. A GPIO button or ESP32 EN/reset cannot power-cycle the converter.

USB CAN PREVENT A FULL POWER CYCLE
USB plugged into the ESP32 can power it independently of the battery switch.
Disconnect it when checking OFF behavior. A generic clone's simultaneous USB and
external 5 V power isolation is not established. The charging USB and programming
USB should be physically identifiable in the enclosure.

IF YOU REALLY NEED TO REPLICATE UNPLUGGING THE CELL
A separate master disconnect can interrupt CELL POSITIVE before the charger's B+
terminal. All project power must still pass through the protected OUT terminals.
With charging and programming USB unplugged, this electrically replicates opening
the battery connection. It also disconnects the battery from charging while OFF.
If charging USB remains connected, the charger can still provide energy to its
output/load; opening the battery lead alone is not a guaranteed all-power cutoff.

Therefore the normal user switch should be on protected OUT+ before the boost;
retain access to a keyed battery connector or a separate service disconnect if
needed. If only a cell disconnect recovers the device after a proper protected-
output power cycle, a charger/protection latch or another power-path issue is
possible. A power switch can provide recovery but does not establish or remove
the underlying cause.

ENCLOSURE FEATURES TO RESERVE
- Front: LCD window/bezel and encoder bushing/knob clearance, including full push
  travel and comfortable finger clearance. Mount the encoder by its bushing when
  possible so force does not load solder joints.
- Side: accessible maintained power switch; protected charging USB opening; a
  separate ESP32 programming USB opening or removable service cover.
- Cable exit: a clamp/grommet and internal slack so camera-cable pulls do not reach
  the solder joints. A panel-mounted connector can serve the same purpose.
- Inside: standoffs matched to the actual PCB holes; insulation/clearance under
  perfboard joints; battery restraint without crushing a pouch or loading its leads;
  clearance around charger/converter components for heat dissipation.
- Service access: battery connector, ESP32 EN/BOOT buttons, and test points should
  be reachable without cutting or desoldering wires.

No exact printable CAD or cutout dimensions are included because PCB outlines,
mounting holes, encoder thread, switch body, USB position and battery dimensions
have not been measured. Measure those actual parts before fixing the enclosure
size; nominal screen diagonal is not enough to size its window or mounting.
